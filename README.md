## Overview

Sauce & Spoon is a growing restaurant chain with sales up 28% a year, but flat profit margins, order delivery times up 20%, guest reviews citing "delay in service," and front-of-house turnover at 50%. Leadership wants to know whether guest-facing tabletop menu tablets can serve more guests in less time and produce the data needed to support expansion.

As the company's first in-house project manager, I led the initiation of a **pilot in the bar sections of two restaurants (North and Downtown)**, running from April through the end of June. I defined the project, set measurable goals, analyzed the stakeholders, and resolved conflicting expectations before any work began.

> **Note:** This is a learning project. Sauce & Spoon, its team, and its figures come from the course scenario and are fictional. The cost figures come from the course's vendor quote. The benefit estimates are my own illustrative assumptions and are labeled as such in the charter.

## Key goals

- Cut average bar table turn time by about 30 minutes (from about 80 to about 50) by June 30
- Increase daily guest count by 10%
- Increase appetizer sales: +10% at North and +20% at Downtown
- Raise the average check from $65 to at least $75
- Reduce food waste by 25%
- Integrate with the existing POS and host systems, train all bar staff before launch, and monitor kitchen capacity

## Highlights from the work

- **Resolved conflicting scope.** The executive chef wanted a change to the order-return policy included in the project, while one general manager disagreed. I moved it to a separate operations discussion and documented how changes would come back through change control.
- **Caught a conflict in the source documents.** The proposal said to "implement a POS system," but the meeting described integrating with the existing one. I clarified the scope and recorded the decision.
- **Turned vague goals into measurable ones.** For example, "increase appetizer sales" became location-specific targets measured as revenue per guest, so a rise in guest count isn't counted twice.
- **Built an honest cost-benefit case.** At $50,550, the pilot is not expected to pay for itself within its 12 weeks. Payback is about 32 weeks in the base case, or about 19 weeks if the $75 average check goal is met.
- **Tailored communication to power and interest.** For example, the sponsor gets short milestone updates, while the kitchen managers help with day-to-day kitchen details so the executive chef isn't pulled into every decision.
