#  Sauce & Spoon Tabletop Menu Tablet Pilot

A project management portfolio piece (Google Project Management Certificate). I acted as the project manager for a pilot rollout of tabletop menu tablets at a restaurant chain, and produced the initiation and planning documents below.

> **Note:** Sauce & Spoon, its team, and its figures come from a course scenario and are fictional. Costs come from the course's vendor quote. Benefit estimates are my own illustrative assumptions and are labeled that way in the charter.

##  Executive Summary

* **The Challenge:** Sauce & Spoon's sales grew 28% a year, but profit margins stayed flat, order delivery times rose 20%, reviews cited "delay in service," and front-of-house turnover reached 50%. Leadership needed to know if tabletop tablets could serve more guests faster and produce data for expansion.
* **The Solution:** I scoped a low-risk pilot in the bar sections of two restaurants (April to June), defined measurable goals, mapped the stakeholders, and resolved conflicting expectations before work began.
* **Targets the pilot is built to prove:** bar table turn time down about 30 minutes, daily guests up 10%, average check from $65 to at least $75, and food waste down 25%, on a $50,550 budget.

##  Highlights

* **Resolved conflicting scope.** The executive chef wanted an order-return policy change added to the project, and a general manager disagreed. I moved it to a separate operations discussion, with changes coming back through change control.
* **Caught a conflict in the source documents.** The proposal said to implement a POS system, but the meeting described integrating with the existing one. I clarified the scope and recorded the decision.
* **Made vague goals measurable.** "Increase appetizer sales" became location-specific targets measured as revenue per guest, so growth in guest count isn't counted twice.
* **Built an honest cost-benefit case.** The pilot isn't expected to pay for itself in its 12 weeks (about 32 weeks to payback, or about 19 if the $75 average check goal is met).
* **Matched communication to influence.** The owner gets short milestone updates, while the kitchen managers handle day-to-day kitchen details so the executive chef isn't pulled into every decision.
