## 1. Project Summary

Sauce & Spoon is a growing restaurant chain with 28% annual sales growth but flat profit margins, order delivery times up 20%, reviews citing "delay in service," and front-of-house turnover of 50%. The project installed guest-facing tabletop menu tablets at two locations (North and Downtown), connected to the existing POS and host software, to serve more guests faster and give leadership the data to decide on a chain-wide rollout.

**Key success criteria**
- Cut average table turn time by 30 minutes
- Increase daily guest count by 10%
- Reduce food waste by 25%
- Fewer than 5% of tablet-using guests report technical issues each week
- Checkout of one minute or less, and 98% order accuracy
- Lobby wait of 10 minutes or less
- Stay within the $50,550 vendor quote

## 2. Methodology

The project used a **hybrid approach**.

- **Traditional (plan-driven) for initiation and planning.** I wrote a project charter, analyzed stakeholders with a power-interest grid, and rewrote vague goals as SMART goals. The project plan used a work breakdown with milestones, task dependencies, three-point (PERT) estimates, a critical path and a launch-date buffer. Quality standards, evaluation questions and evaluation indicators were defined before launch.
- **Agile-style iteration after the test launch.** A test launch with 50 guests, a customer survey and a retrospective let the team find problems, adjust (navigation layout, payment messaging, waitstaff training, pre-service testing) and re-measure before the full launch.
- **Ongoing coordination.** Weekly calls with the vendor, Terrific Tablets, kept dependencies clear. Stakeholders were managed by influence and interest, for example short milestone updates for the sponsor and day-to-day kitchen detail through the kitchen managers.
- **Change control** was used for scope questions, such as the order-return policy, which was moved to a separate operations discussion.

## 3. Key Accomplishments

**Did the project achieve its initial goals?** Yes, after adjustments made following the test launch.

| Goal | Target | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Table turn time | 30 minutes faster | **Met** (30 minutes reduced) | Post-launch email |
| Table wait time | Lower as turn time falls | **Met** (shorter waits) | Post-launch email |
| Daily guest count | +10% | **Exceeded** (+20% at Downtown) | Post-launch email |
| Food waste | -25% | **Met** (25% reached) | Post-launch email |
| Tablet checkout time | 1 minute or less | **Met** | Post-launch email |
| Technical issues | Under 5% of guests weekly | **Met** (test launch was 12%) | Post-launch email; survey Q15 |
| Order accuracy | 98% | **Not confirmed** (test launch was 72%) | Survey Q9; post-launch email |
| Appetizer sales; average check ($65 to $75) | +10% North, +20% Downtown; $75 | **Not reported** | Not in sources |
| Budget and schedule | $50,550 | **Not reported** | Not in sources |

**Were problems solved?** Mostly.
- Tablet navigation was confusing for some guests (only 48% rated it easy), so the layout was simplified.
- Table turn time did not improve at first. Training and pacing guidance for waitstaff, worked out with the general managers, fixed it.
- Glitchy tablets (12% of guests had issues) were reduced with a pre-service testing checklist.
- Cash payments confused guests. Clearer messaging now says the tablets take cards only, and cash handling was streamlined.
- Incorrect orders are the open problem. They persisted after the test launch, so the team agreed to look at every possible source, including the kitchen.

**Does the deliverable meet or beat requirements?** On the measured goals, yes. At the test launch, 72% of guests rated the experience 4 or 5 out of 5, 82% found checkout quick, easy and secure, and 70% preferred tablet-only or a tablet and waiter mix. After the adjustments, most targets were met or beaten.

## 4. Lessons Learned

**What went well**
- Weekly vendor calls kept tasks and dependencies clear.
- The kitchen ticket flow worked well, and the kitchen began improving its operations because of it.
- The test launch, survey and retrospective found real problems before the full launch.
- Both locations installed on time and POS integration issues were fixed quickly.

**What did not go well**
- **Planning missed people and place.** Vacation time was unaccounted for, and each location's operational history was not reviewed, which slowed implementation and caused unplanned issues.
- **Table turn time stayed flat at first**, partly because one location prioritized a relaxed experience over the project objective.
- **Orders were still sent back**, and the disagreement over the cause (kitchen versus the send-back policy) became heated.
- **Cash payment was not planned for.** About 10% of guests pay in cash.
- **Birthday Club sign-up was low** (16%), because the program was not clearly explained on the tablets.

**Unforeseen benefits and consequences**
- Benefit: daily guest count rose 20%, double the goal.
- Benefit: the new ticket flow led the kitchen to improve its own operations.
- Consequence: the tablets exposed a gap between front-of-house and back-of-house understanding.
- Consequence: guests who wanted a relaxed meal pushed back on faster turns.

**Processes that did not work well**
- Planning without checking each location's history and staff availability.
- Discussing disputed causes in front of the whole group. Pausing the discussion, restating the shared goal and asking for a commitment to investigate worked better.

**Key problems and their effects**
- Incorrect orders (28% at the test launch): wasted food, remakes and a risk to guest satisfaction and the 4.5-star rating.
- Cash checkout friction (about 10% of guests): slower checkout and a risk of fewer repeat visits and weaker reviews.

## 5. Next Steps

**Ongoing maintenance and development**
- Maintenance and IT support from Terrific Tablets (the quote covers fees through the end of the year).
- Keep the pre-service tablet testing checklist and update the process manual with each issue and fix.
- Update the website and tablet content for the Birthday Club.

**Open action items**
- Find and fix the cause of incorrect orders (Seydou to check technical causes; Carter and the kitchen managers to review kitchen causes).
- Decide on a cash checkout process (for example, asking guests at the start of the meal and adding a second register near the kitchen).
- Back-of-house scale-up before launches at more locations.
- A joint front-of-house and back-of-house session, and a review of waitstaff training (possibly two parts).
- Confirm and record the appetizer sales and average check results, and the final cost against the $50,550 budget.
- Deliver the kitchen staffing recommendation to Deanna.

**Risks to watch next quarter**
- Order errors continue and hurt reviews and food waste results.
- Different location cultures (for example, a relaxed dining pace) slow the table turn gains at new locations.
- Tablet reliability and cash handling problems repeat at new locations.
- Kitchen capacity strain as guest volume rises.

**Ownership and recommendations for future project managers**
- **Ownership:** Molly Edwards takes over the rollout at the remaining locations. Deanna Coleman remains the business lead.
- Review each location's history and staff availability before planning begins.
- Keep the weekly vendor calls.
- Run a test launch, survey and retrospective at each location before the full launch.
- Plan for cash and other payment edge cases from the start.
- Tie requests to shared goals and data, and bring in the kitchen early.
